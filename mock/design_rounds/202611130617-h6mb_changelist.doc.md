This changelist takes into the api's design what kaski's engine needs of hilavitkutin, as revisions and extensions of concepts the design already has. The lead designer asked for it in those terms ("Revise the api in place now") after the branch `work/loop-core` had built the same needs as a second vocabulary beside the scheduler.

It is the second writing of this changelist. The first (as posted at `288811a`) added a frame-buffered store kind, record domains on the column marker and a refusal of a second writer, on the premise that the founding record was silent on them. His answers of 13 November reject that premise in so many words ("have you not read the whole thing?", "Pretty sure the design has answers to these things and more"), and a whole reading of the founding record by this seat and by the seat porting it to the registry bears him out. This writing restates each form on what the record designs, says where the record stops, and adds beyond it only where one of his answers of 13 November asks for more. The section "What changed from the changelist at `288811a`" lists the differences.

The designs here are his and stand in for a canon the repository does not declare yet. So every line this changelist changes is quoted as it stood, with what it becomes and why. No human sat in this round. He reads the result afterwards and may overturn any of it.

The founding record is cited by these names. The spec is `mock/design_rounds/202604200055/202603181200_topic.hilavitkutin-design-consolidation.md`. The core design topic is `202603141800_topic.hilavitkutin-core-design.md`, the api surface topic `202603151200_topic.hilavitkutin-api-surface.md` and the memory layout topic `202603121310_topic.memory-layout-and-platform.md`, all in the same directory. "The api design" is `mock/crates/hilavitkutin-api/DESIGN.md.tmpl` and "the engine design" is `mock/crates/hilavitkutin/DESIGN.md.tmpl`. Line numbers of the two designs are those of the files on the trunk at `a32fa32`, before this changelist.

## Current state

The api's design names three store types (`Resource`, `Column`, `Virtual`), while the source has `Accum` beside them, which the api's design never mentions and the engine's design describes. The scheduler is built with one record count (`build(storage, record_count)`) and every column has that many records, though `ColumnStorage` already keeps a count per column.

A unit is a `WorkUnit`. It iterates through `each`, `batch` or `reduce` with a closure over a position, and reads and writes columns through `unsafe fn read` and `unsafe fn write` at that position. `RecordOp` exists beside it as an opt-in map of one value to one value, used only by the fused fold. The founding record has `each` take a function from values to values (the api surface topic, `:1840-1843`), which the shipped `each` does not.

A unit reads the record it is at and no other. The engine design has units ranked by lifecycle (plan-stage, consumer, epilogue) through `OnMeta<V>`, and plan step 1 builds a read-after-write edge from any writer to any reader of a store whatever their ranks, so a store written on the epilogue band and read by a consumer unit gets an edge against its rank.

Two writers of one store are ordered by registration position alone in the engine design (`:43-44`) and in the code, where the spec has hint priority first.

The type-level list is declared in the api (`Cons`, `Empty`, `Contains`, `ContainsAll`, `Concat`), a second copy of what `notko-hlist` now holds.

Formats are generic: any `Copy + 'static` type is a `ColumnValue`. Registration is typed, through `.with`. Neither changes.

## Changes proposed

The first eight follow the eight things the coordinator asked to have restated on the record, in its numbering. The remaining ones carry over from the first writing.

### 1. Reading a store as it stood when the pass began, and what the counters are for

What the record designs. Hilavitkutin has two access modes and no buffered store. The core design topic, `:643-661`:

> Resolved by Q2: two modes only. Read and Write. Write implies Read.
>
> loimu's seven access modes (Pinned, Immediate, MutImmediate, Sample, Post, Approx, Defer) control ordering, freshness, and determinism within the double-buffered view system. [...] loimu maps its seven modes to Read/Write at the hilavitkutin boundary and implements the richer semantics in its own layer.
>
> If hilavitkutin later needs a third mode (e.g. ReadExclusive for consumers that need guaranteed exclusive read access), it can be added without changing the WorkUnit trait — it would be a new store wrapper type like `Exclusive<Resource<T>>` in the AccessSet.

The counters are for one thing, knowing whether something changed so that unchanged work is skipped. The spec, `:2406-2408` (R2): "hilavitkutin owns per-morsel generation counters and skip-unchanged DAG propagation logic (runtime scheduling concern)". No passage has a counter select or hold an earlier value of a store.

What the record also has is the band a frame-start read falls out of. The engine design's meta pipeline section ranks every unit by its schedule: "plan-stage work units land in the early phase(s), consumers in the middle, and epilogue (`ScheduleEnd`) work units in the final phase(s)". A column whose every writer is on the epilogue band therefore holds, for every consumer unit of a pass, what it held when the pass began.

What changes. No store kind and no read marker is added. The api design gains the section "Maintenance units and the close" with the subsection "Reading a store as it stood when the pass began", which says the above: the frozen read follows from rank, a whole-field step is two declared columns and a close unit that carries the second into the first, both the consumer's, and the counters select no value. It also says the engine may later rotate the two columns' storage in place of the copy with neither declaration changing.

Two engine rules make it hold. The engine design, `:41-43`:

> RAW edges are order-independent: for any unit pair where one writes a store the other reads, the writer sorts before the reader, regardless of which was registered first.

becomes: order-independent between units of one lifecycle rank, and two units of different rank that share a store take the order of their ranks with no edge built against it. Without this, step 1 orders a close-band writer before a consumer reader of the same store, against the bands.

The engine design, `:106-110`, lists the seeds of the dirty set (a replaced resource, a replaced value, "an input the consumer marked changed", the cold first frame). It gains one: a store a close unit changed in the pass before. This is a new seed, the one the engine takes from its own units, and it is stated as a change. A consumer unit that read a store before a close unit wrote it has not seen that write, so the write makes the store's readers dirty for the next pass.

His answer, with the options it chose among. Asked how a unit reads a store's frame-start value [a: ordinary column plus a read marker "as of frame start" | b: one kind with a committed and a staged side swapped by a meta unit, writers ordered like any | c: two ordinary stores and a carry unit on the frame-end meta band | d: as the revision wrote], he answered: "I am almost 100% we have these counters/increments already designed exactly for something like this. Hilavitkutin has a spec and known downstream users (not us at least not yet) that do triple buffering even and the spec should answer that already at least in intent… have you not read the whole thing? Perhaps you better increase your efforts on porting the canon and design in hila to a registry so canon tools and such can be used on it, as unearth and already said too and whatever else we are upstrwaming". The intent in it, in my words: the spec already answers how a consumer that buffers two or three deep sits on hilavitkutin, and the revision must be written from it. The rest is kept for provenance. What the record gives is the answer above, which has the shape of his option c, and it does not give the counters the job of selecting a value; the section on disagreements says so with both passages.

Where the record stops. Who rotates. The record has loimu buffering in its own layer and a research note of the same round rating its swap buffer as portable to hilavitkutin, which the spec did not take up. The other seat has put that to him. This changelist takes the reading that needs nothing added, the consumer's two columns and a carry, and leaves the rotation as plumbing a later round may add.

### 2. Columns of different record counts

What the record designs. A column is `Column<T>` and the schema is the consumer's. The spec, `:2397-2404` (R1): "hilavitkutin doesn't know or care about domain schemas — it schedules based on Read/Write column sets only", with the typed schema pattern "consumer-side" as a "consumer wrapper around hilavitkutin's `Column<T>`". Record counts are run-time parameters of the plan stage (the spec, `:2110-2117`: "The plan stage adjusts only what compile time cannot: morsel sizes from runtime hardware detection, record counts, EMA-based recomputation"), they come from the consumer's data (`:2435-2446`, R6), and a count belongs to a fiber's run of work (the memory layout topic, `:854-863`: "the row count cascades: if the upstream chain processed N rows, the downstream chain also processes N rows", in that topic's older vocabulary). The storage contract already answers per column: `ColumnStorage::count(id)`, and a second `reserve` of an id resizes it (`mock/crates/hilavitkutin-api/src/storage.rs:38-80`).

What changes. `Column<T>` keeps one parameter. The api design gains the section "Record counts" after the store wrappers: a column's count is a fact of its storage, a column registered with `Column::<T>::new()` holds the run configuration's record count as every column did, a column that holds another count says so where it is registered, a unit's columns hold one count, and a unit whose columns hold different counts is refused as a value at `build` where the plan reads the counts. A fiber's units share one count. `build(storage, record_count)` is unchanged.

The engine design, `:269`: "a `morsel_local` fiber windows `[0, record_count)`" becomes `[0, n)` with `n` the count the fiber's columns hold. At `:1134-1141` the `Column<T>` drain arm gains: a column registered bare is reserved at the frame record count, and one registered with a count of its own is reserved at its capacity with its count as the live count.

His answers. Asked how columns of differing record counts are held [a: on the column in storage, a unit over unequal columns refused | b: on the column's type as a group parameter | c: consumer wrappers name groups], he answered: "Okay so i am fairway confident this is also answered. Isn’t the intent a contiguous per prototype and indices to process needed windows to it? Pretty sure the design has answers to these things and more". The intent, in my words: the design already holds this, as contiguous storage per kind of thing and windows of indices over it. That is the record's morsel over a column's own count, and it is what the section states. Asked whether a domain's extent is fixed or replaceable [a: replaceable between passes up to a capacity fixed at build | b: fixed at build | c: both, declared per domain in its type], he answered: "Both, declared per domain".

Where the record stops, and what is added under that last answer. The record has no way to say that a column's count is fixed or replaceable. Added, and narrow: `Column::<T>::with_count(n)` for a fixed count and `Column::<T>::with_capacity(cap, n)` for one replaceable up to a capacity reserved at build, both returning `Counted<Column<T>>`, a registration of the same store that stages an `Extent` (a plain value, a count and a capacity). `with_capacity` asks `T: Replaceable`, so which of the two a column is declared in its value type, by the opt-in marker a replaced resource value already asks. The scheduler gains `replace_count`, which takes a list of columns and one count between passes and refuses, as a value, a count past a capacity or one that would leave a unit's columns unequal. "Per domain" is read as per column here because the record keeps the grouping of columns consumer-side (R1), so there is no domain in the api to hang the declaration on. That reading is flagged for the coordinator.

Not in this revision: a unit reading a column of another count at a record it computes (a lookup by key). Idea ticket `a_readable_member_that_gives_a_record_its_own_key` stands for it.

### 3. The order of two writers of one store

What the record designs. The spec, `:589-595`:

> - DAG edges from Write→Read column overlap
> - Write-write ambiguity resolved by scheduling hint priority (Urgency, Divisibility, Significance). Tie-break: most deps first, then deterministic fallback. Supersedes T6 L2637 `order` field.

One rule for every store, and no store has a refusal in place of an order.

His answers. Asked about two units writing one frame-buffered column [a: refused at build now, later disjoint record sets | b: always refused at build | c: resolved by scheduling priority like every other kind], he answered: "Schedule should exist for this specifically. Are we sure that you and the agents working on this actually know what hilavitkutin is and is intended to be?" The intent, in my words: ordering writers is what the schedule is for, and nothing is refused. Asked for the write-write order [a: hint priority, most dependents, fixed fallback as founded; registration order a deviation to fix | b: hint priority, then most dependents, with registration position named as the fixed deterministic fallback | c: registration position, founding rule retired], he answered: "Hints, registration as fallback". That is read as option b, whose text spells the middle step. His own words name the first and the last step only, and the middle one is kept from the spec.

What changes. The api design, `:366-368`:

> Write-write ambiguity resolved by scheduling hint priority across three dimensions. Tie-break: most deps first, then deterministic fallback.

becomes: two units that write one store are ordered by the schedule, for every store; hint priority across the three dimensions decides first, then the unit with more dependents downstream, then the position the units were registered in, which is the deterministic fallback; no second writer is refused.

The engine design, `:43-44`:

> WAW conflicts serialise deterministically by input index (the lower index first).

becomes the same rule, with the input index as its last step. The code orders by input index alone (`mock/crates/hilavitkutin/src/plan/steps/step1_dag.rs:49`), a deviation from the spec that predates this round, ticket `hilavitkutin_orders_two_writers_of_one_store_by_registration`. The source window either carries hint priority into step 1 or leaves a FIXME there naming that ticket.

### 4. Who declares what changed means

What the record designs. The engine owns the counters, the stamps and the skip (R2, quoted under 1), and "Version stamps increment only on completion" (the core design topic, `:375-377`). Whether a write that stored the same value counts is not the engine's to fix. The loimu research of the founding round, `mock/design_rounds/202604200055/202603141800_research.loimu-dag.md:1021-1025`:

> **Version stamps:** the concept is universal (track whether a resource actually changed). hilavitkutin should own the version stamp type and the early cutoff algorithm. Phantom-write detection (bitwise comparison for small values, explicit marking for large values) is a storage concern that each consumer can implement differently.

His answers. Asked what "changed" means for a computed column [a: differs from the frame before, compared with the committed value at a granularity fixed in the contract | b: written this frame, whatever the value | c: declared per column: the column or the unit writing it declares which of the two holds, as a trait or const, default differs], he answered: "Declared per column". Asked which stores declare it [a: every per-record kind, default differs for frame-buffered and written for in-place | b: frame-buffered kind only | c: every per-record kind, default differs everywhere | d: the writing unit declares it, not the store], he answered: "Last option. Pretty sure this is somewhere in the research and proposed canon…". The intent, in my words: the unit that writes a column declares, for that column, what a change is.

What changes. The api design gains "What counts as changed" under "Members of an access set". The declaration is on the member the unit writes. A plain `Column<T>` in a write list counts as changed by the unit wherever the unit wrote it. `Differs<Column<T>>` counts as changed only where the returned value differs from the value the record held, compared by the engine beside the store, and asks `T: PartialEq`. No store carries a rule, and two units may write one column under different declarations.

The engine design, `:104-117`, gains that the close band's seed (under 1) reads this declaration, that the mark is per store in this revision, and, under what is left for later, that within a pass the skip stays structural.

Where the record stops. The form of the declaration and its default. The default kept is "written": the option he chose from says "default differs", but differs as the default would add a compare and a `PartialEq` bound to every write that exists, which breaks every unit whose value type cannot be compared. Flagged for the coordinator as a choice that may be his.

### 5. A cross-record read with a reach

What the record designs. Records within a fiber are independent (the spec, `:609-613`: "Records within a fiber are independent", "Execution order of records doesn't matter for correctness"), work that needs all of its input before its first output sits behind a boundary (`:1089-1093`: "No pipeline breaker: ops requiring all input records before output → materialization boundary."), and a need the two modes do not cover enters as "a new store wrapper type" in the access set (the core design topic, quoted under 1). The record has no read of a neighbouring record.

His answer. Asked whether hilavitkutin owns geometry [a: no | b: an opt-in hilavitkutin crate over the api's declared cross-record read | c: yes, in the api], he answered: "Opt-in crate over the api".

What changes. The api design gains "A read with a reach" under "Members of an access set": `Reach<Column<T>, N>` as a read-list member whose value is a `Window` with `here`, `before` and `after`, the last two giving nothing past either end of the column, and an offset past `N` failing to compile. The reach is a count of records along the column's one sequence. Axes, shapes, neighbours in two dimensions and edge values are not in the api. Ticket `an_opt_in_hilavitkutin_crate_gives_axes_grid_shapes_and_edge` holds the crate, which is not in this job.

What the declaration is for is that the plan knows the read is not record-independent. A unit that reads a column with a reach does not write it (a const assertion at `build`), and a read-after-write edge into such a read is a phase boundary, so every writer's whole range has landed on every core before the reader starts. The engine design's new subsection says both, and step 9 (`:75-89`) gains that a reach adds nothing to the write-only sizing.

`N` is a const parameter of type `Cap`, the type `Seq` and `Map` take their capacities in (`mock/crates/hilavitkutin-api/src/store.rs:209`), so no bare primitive is taken as a const parameter. The first writing's `Shift<A, const N: isize>` did take one, which the review asked about.

Where the record stops. Everything about the reach itself is new under his answer. A reach known only at run time is not covered.

### 6. The accumulator

What the record designs. The accumulator topic, `mock/design_rounds/202606041800/202606041800_topic.accumulator-columns.md:8`:

> An accumulator is written by APPEND during the frame, growing from zero toward a capacity; a later PassEnd drain reads the populated prefix into persistence. op chose this two-kind surface deliberately rather than a single global record count.

What changes. The api design, `:138-152`:

> Three store types, one scheduling mechanism.

stays "Three store types, one scheduling mechanism", and gains "and the accumulator as a column whose write is an append", with `Accum<T>` listed in the code block and a paragraph on it. The first writing changed the count to five and the heading to "Store kinds"; both are taken back. Whether the accumulator makes a fourth store type is an open row of the registry the other seat is porting, and this changelist does not answer it: it names the marker the source has had since June and says what it is.

`append` stays safe as the first writing made it, with `len` beside it; see 9.

Added under his word on maintenance units (the next section): a reader of the pass's appends for a close unit. The record's only reader is the PassEnd drain into persistence, which the accumulator round left out of its scope (`:42`).

### The close and maintenance units

This is not one of the eight. It is asked for by a word of his from the kaski canon walk, relayed by the coordinator: "mutations are NOT writes, in this sense. Write here refers to in-place writes that are discouraged or forbidden at the contract level and forces in-engine / sim changes to go through mutations. Special contracts exist f or work units that are maintenance (can write in place, primarily do frame close and setup and such)". The intent, in my words: an ordinary unit does not write in place, its changes go through mutations, and the units that write in place are maintenance units, which do the setup and the close of a frame.

What the record gives maintenance units. The spec, `:2119-2137`, has the scheduler schedule itself through meta units on the meta virtuals `PlanStage`, `ScheduleReady`, `PassStart` and `ScheduleEnd`. The engine design's meta pipeline section has them in the same carrier as consumer units, ranked by schedule type at const evaluation, scheduled with `OnMeta<V>` where a consumer writes `On<V>`, with a `meta` accessor that exists only on the context of a meta unit ("enforced at compile time by the gating, not by a separate bound"), and with the meta bands run once per frame on one core over the whole range. The record gives them no in-place write of their own and no reader of the pass's appends: every unit has the same `unsafe fn write`.

What changes. The api design gains "Maintenance units and the close". A unit scheduled `OnMeta<V>` is a maintenance unit; one on a plan-stage virtual or `PassStart` sets a pass up, one on `ScheduleEnd` is the close. An ordinary unit writes what its function returns at its own record, and appends, so a change it means for another record leaves it as an appended value. A maintenance unit writes in place with the positional `write(i, v)` and applies what the pass gathered. It reads the appends through `AccumReaderApi`, an accessor a runner implements on the context of a maintenance unit alone, the way the `meta` accessor is gated, so no bound of `WorkUnit::Ctx` changes.

`OnMeta<V>` is added to the api design's list of schedule conditions (just above `:366`), which named `Always` and `On<V>` only.

What is not done. `write` is not taken away from an ordinary unit. Every positional unit in the suites calls it, and the brief's item 9 asks that the existing parts keep working. It stays `unsafe` for everyone, and the design says a consumer that forbids in-place writes outside its maintenance units keeps its ordinary units in crates that forbid `unsafe`. The stricter contract, `write` existing only on a maintenance context by the same gating, would match his word more closely and would break every positional unit. It is put to the coordinator as an open choice.

### 7. Content adding stores

Nothing changes. Typed registration through `.with` stays the only registration in the contract, and the record has every store declared in Rust. His answer on a run-time, data-bound binding table [typed only for now | both, typed first | decide in the api round, which brings a sketch and a bench of both] was "Decide in the api round". The sketch (`mock/research/sketches/202611130728_data-bound-registration/`), the bench (`registration_binding` in `mock/benches/bench.toml`) and the note (`mock/research/202611130728_data-bound-registration-findings.md`) are done and say: a table consulted once a morsel is inside the noise of two builds of one source; a lookup and a call per record costs seven to eight times the typed loop; a stride known only at run time costs 75% to 87% over it. ### 8. The value function, relative to the work unit

What the record designs. The work unit is the one unit of code the scheduler knows, and the function over values is what its `each` takes. The api surface topic, `:1840-1843`:

> The WU's `each` closure signature changes from `|col_a: &T, col_b: &mut U| { ... }` to a pure function `fn(input_a: T, input_b: T) -> (U, V)` that takes values and returns values. The flattener wires the returns to the next stage's inputs.

and the core design topic, `:375-377`: "**No partial writes.** Work units buffer internally, write on return."

What changes. The api design, `:239-243`, the section "Opt-in per-record transform (within-fiber fusion contract)":

> A work unit that is a pure per-record map (reads one column, writes one column, value to value, no cross-record state) may additionally declare that map as an opt-in fusion contract: an associated input type, an associated output type, and an apply function from one to the other. This is orthogonal to the execute contract (it does not change execute) and opt-in (a simple work unit never implements it).

is replaced by "The value function". `EachApi` gains `map` beside `run`: the unit hands `each` a function from the values of its read list at one record to the values of its write list at that record, from `execute`, which stays the one entry point. The engine stores what the function returns. `WorkUnit` is unchanged, and so is `Plannable`, which stays the one declaration the plan reads; the sentence at `:403-404` ("The plan stage reads `Plannable`, never `WorkUnit::execute`, so planning is generic over `Plannable<S>`.") stands.

`RecordOp` is kept as the same function under a name on the unit's type, for the fold that cannot see into a closure. It moves under `WorkUnit` (`pub trait RecordOp<S = Always>: WorkUnit<S>`) and its `apply` is widened from one value in and one out to the unit's own lists. The fused fold still takes one-column maps (the engine design, `:361-363`, says so in the new terms).

A member's value is given by two traits, `Readable` and `Writable`, over the members of 4 and 5 and the store markers. A resource is read by value or by reference by its kind, through `ResourceRead`, which the `ResourceFootprint` derive writes from the fields it already sizes. His answer on that, asked [a: by reference, the read product carries a lifetime | b: by value, only `Copy` resources, collections through the block form | c: both, by the resource's kind, chosen in the types]: "Yes. Use typestate and statically branch accordingly", read as c.

The `WorkUnit` code block of the api design (`:327`) shows `type Hint` with a default the source does not have (`mock/crates/hilavitkutin-api/src/work_unit.rs:54-97`); the default is removed from the block and a sentence says a unit names its hint. The review of the first writing found this.

Where the record stops. The record's function takes and returns bare values; the products over the lists, `rec!`, `r.get(MEMBER)` and the member traits are this round's. A unit still names its context type in `Ctx`, as on the trunk, so a unit written as a value function is not yet free of the runner's name. The first writing's routers bought that and are withdrawn because the record puts the function inside the work unit. Flagged for the coordinator.

### 9. Positional reads, writes and appends

The api design, `:436-443`, had `unsafe fn read<T: ColumnValue, I>(&self, i: USize) -> T` and `unsafe fn write<T: ColumnValue, I>(&self, i: USize, v: T)`, bounded on `Contains`. They stay as they were, bounded on `Holds` (10). The sentence at `:527-528` ("Raw-pointer access `unsafe read / write` pinky-swears ownership discipline that the engine proves at plan time.") stands. The `&self` receiver on every provider method stays, as the review of the index sketch asked.

The first writing made `read` and `write` safe through a branded `Index<'b>` minted by `each`, with `Block<'b>`, `PerRecord`, `InPlace` and `Index::mint`. All of it is withdrawn. The safe write of a unit's own record is the value function's (8), which the record designed, and a maintenance unit's write lands at a record named by data, which a cursor-minted index cannot serve. The review's faults in that form (the mint public, the index `Copy` and so no proof of one append, `batch` losing the position as a number) fall away with it. The sketch `202611130417_branded-morsel-index` stays as a record of the form.

`append` is made safe, as in the first writing, and for its reason: the api design's own refusal paragraph ends on "no caller obligation covers the capacity", and what is left of the obligation is the one appender slot the plan proves. `len` stays beside it.

### 10. The list is the shared crate's

Unchanged from the first writing. The api design, `:159-163`:

> `AccessSet` is a sealed marker on the cons-list typestate. `Empty` and `Cons<H, T>` are the substrate primitives consumer code encounters either directly or through the `read!` / `write!` macros (which expand to nested `Cons` chains).

becomes: `AccessSet` is the api's bound over the type-level list of `notko-hlist`; `Empty`, `Cons`, `Contains`, `ContainsAll` and `Concat` are that crate's, the first two re-exported from the api's root. ## How each form holds the pillars

His words of 13 November bound every change to hilavitkutin: it may be "added and extended and changed" while it does not compromise "full, exhaustive typestate, contracts first, all machinery and logic runs on contracts alone, people impl its parts and the engine runs it via the contracts", with "optimisations first and foremost, always", started simpler and deepened later, and nothing "a drop looser in typestate or strictness with no std, no alloc, unless behind a feature that is entirely opt in".

The value function. In the types: each read bounded on the read list, the return total over the write list. Contract: `EachApi::map`, `Readable`, `Writable`, and `RecordOp` under `WorkUnit`. Optimisation: the function is pure over values, so the engine is free in the sequence of records, the thread and in composing units; in this revision it runs one gather, call and scatter per record and fuses only one-column maps.

Members and wrappers. In the types: a wrapper is a type in the list, `Reach` only readable and `Differs` only writable, the reach a const parameter, an offset past it a compile error. Contract: `Member`, with `AccessSet` over members. Optimisation: the plan knows a cross-record read and a change rule at build, which is what a boundary, a halo and a skip need.

Record counts. In the types: whether a count may be replaced is `Replaceable` on the value type. Not in the types: that a unit's columns hold one count, since counts are run-time values; it is a value refused at `build` and at `replace_count`. Contract: `ColumnStorage` as it is, `Counted`, `Extent`. Optimisation: a fiber still walks one contiguous range.

Maintenance units and the close. In the types: the schedule `OnMeta<V>` is the unit's rank, and the accumulator reader exists only on a maintenance context. Not in the types: an ordinary unit can still call the `unsafe` positional `write`. Contract: `WorkUnit<OnMeta<V>>`, `AccumReaderApi`. Optimisation: none gained; the close band is one core for now.

The shared list. As in the first writing: the same sealed list and marker membership, `Holds` and `HoldsAll` over it.

Nothing above needs `std` or alloc, none of it is behind a feature, and no unstable feature is added to either crate.
