# The sixth route, and what a shape may still decide

**Date:** 2026-08-10
**Phase:** TOPIC
**Scope:** `hilavitkutin-sym`

The ninth review found two more live routes to the collision. Both verified.

**`id_from_counter` is a trait method a shape controls totally**, sitting in the
same counter-to-id-space position `origin_base` occupied before round thirteen
moved it out. A shape whose widths sum to 32, whose `ID_BITS` equals
`ORIGIN_BITS + COUNTER_BITS`, whose declared widths match its layout, and whose
`origin_index` is injective and in range, still mints byte-identical handles from
two distinct minters. Round thirteen moved one escape hatch and left its twin.

**`origin_index` may exceed `2^ORIGIN_BITS`.** The derived runs are then disjoint
in `u32` and truncate to the same id. The law that would catch it,
`origin_indices_fit_origin_bits`, is **private to `mod tests`** and runs against
three shipped shapes, so nothing a consumer writes can reach it. `tests/origins.rs`
says this case is fixed.

## The pattern, at its sixth instance

Every fix so far has removed one numeric freedom from a shape and left another.
Widths, then masks, then bases, then ceilings, then indices, now the counter
projection. Each round closed the route the previous review found, and each left
the class open, because the class is not any particular route: **it is that a
shape is trusted with arithmetic in the id space at all.**

Stating that plainly is overdue. The instrument has been a law aimed at the
construction most recently found, five times, and each law was correct and
insufficient in the same way.

## What this round does, and what it deliberately does not claim

Two changes remove freedom, and one adds a refusal at the point of use.

`id_from_counter` leaves `SymShape`. The projection belongs to the layout, whose
`Id` type its bitfield fixes, so what is left there is forwarding rather than
arithmetic. **This reduces the freedom rather than eliminating it**: a layout is
still written by hand and can still lie, and what constrains it is the round-trip
law that already exists. Round thirteen claimed elimination and was wrong; this
round claims a reduction and names what remains.

`Generator::at` becomes fallible and refuses an origin whose index is out of
range. That closes the second route **at the point of use**, for every shape
including ones nobody has written, which is the only place a check can be
unconditional when the shape is an open extension point.

The fit check stops being a private test helper and becomes a public predicate a
consumer can call on their own shape.

## The question this round does not answer

Whether a shape should be trusted with any id-space arithmetic, or whether the
extension point should narrow to widths and an origin type with everything
numeric derived. Five rounds of evidence say the open version keeps reopening the
defect it was built alongside.

That is a design fork with real cost either way and it goes to op rather than
being decided here. It does not block this round: the changes above are right
under either answer.
