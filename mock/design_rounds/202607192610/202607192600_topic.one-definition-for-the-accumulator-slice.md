# Topic: one definition for the accumulator slice

**Date:** 2026-07-19
**Phase:** TOPIC
**Scope:** `scheduler/mod.rs:957` and `:1920`, the two computations of the per-core accumulator
record slice.
**Grounded on:** roadmap r7 band 1 item R2d; canon `202606111800:452-456`.

The accumulator's per-core regioning is canonical: "each core gets an exclusive region of the
accumulator ... the cores append into their regions in parallel, and the main thread merges the
regions after the workers rejoin, preserving append order". The engine implements it, correctly, and
computes the region boundaries twice.

`worker_accum_unit_outer:957` derives `per = ceil(total / ncores)`, then `lo`, `hi` and `region`,
and rebases the accumulator to `[lo, lo + region)`. `run_parallel:1920` recomputes `per` and passes
it to `merge_accums`, which reads core c's region at offset `c * per`.

## The hazard, stated at its real size

The two must agree exactly. If they ever disagree, the merge reads regions the workers did not write
and the output is silently wrong: no panic, no assert, a frame that completes with corrupt
accumulator contents.

A first draft of the roadmap entry claimed they read different inputs, the worker via a parameter and
the merge via `record_count`. That was wrong and is worth stating rather than quietly dropping: they
share a source, since `:864` is `let total = s.record_count`. The risk is not that they disagree
today.

The risk is that nothing prevents them from disagreeing tomorrow. Two copies of one formula, in
functions eight hundred lines apart, with the invariant that binds them written down nowhere.
Removing the second copy is not cleanup; it is converting a maintenance assumption into a structural
one.
