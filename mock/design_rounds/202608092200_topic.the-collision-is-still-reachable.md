# The collision is still reachable, through the extension point that fixed it

**Date:** 2026-08-09
**Phase:** TOPIC
**Scope:** `hilavitkutin-sym`, `docs/`, `README.md`

This branch exists to stop two peers minting the same handle and comparing
**equal** rather than failing to match. The seventh review found that a shape may
still do exactly that, and I reproduced it.

## The hole

`origin_base`, `origin_ceiling` and `id_from_counter` are typed `Uint<28, Hot>`
**in the trait**. Twenty-eight is `Standard`'s id width, hardcoded at the one
place a shape cannot override, and nothing ties it to the shape's own `ID_BITS`.
`Uint::<28>::from_raw` does not truncate, so a shape may lay its origins out for
a twenty-eight bit space while its `Id` is narrower.

Constructed on `WideKind`, whose `Id` is twenty-six bits: origins shifted by
twenty-four, ceilings a full twenty-two-bit counter above each base,
`ORIGIN_BITS` four, `COUNTER_BITS` twenty-two, `ID_BITS` twenty-six,
`KIND_BITS` five.

Every declaration agrees. **All six shape laws pass.** Origin four's base is
`4 << 24`, which is 2^26, one past what a twenty-six bit id holds, so it
truncates to zero. Origins zero and four mint the identical handle, 67108864,
and two peers holding them compare equal.

That is the defect this branch was opened for, reachable through the mechanism
this branch added to fix it.

## Why every existing law misses it

Each one relates a shape's declarations to each other or to its layout's types.
None relates an origin's **base** to the id field it has to fit in, and none
asserts the property the crate actually promises, which is that two origins
never produce one handle.

The disjointness that is tested is `SixteenMinters`-specific, in
`tests/origins.rs`, over a shape that happens to be laid out correctly. A
shape-generic law would have caught this the moment `WideKind` gained an origin.

The general form is the one this crate keeps relearning, at its fifth and now
sixth instance: **a law over declarations is not a law over behaviour.** Six laws
agreeing about widths did not stop two minters colliding, because none of them
ever minted anything.

## What the round adds

A law that every origin's base and ceiling fit that shape's own `ID_BITS`, and a
**shape-generic disjointness law** that mints from every origin of every shape
and asserts no two handles are equal. The second is the crate's promise stated as
a test rather than as prose, and it is what should have existed from the first
round.

The `Uint<28, Hot>` in the trait signature is the deeper problem and is named
here rather than fixed: it is `Standard`'s width standing where a shape's width
belongs. Whether it can be made to follow the shape is a design question this
round does not have the answer to, and the laws above close the hole meanwhile.

## Also outstanding, from the same review

`Sym<S>`'s derived `PartialEq` carries `S: PartialEq + Eq + Hash + Default +
Debug` from the derive, while `SymShape` requires only `Copy + 'static`. A legal
shape that derives less yields a `Sym` with **no equality at all**, which is the
crate's central promise absent by construction. All three shipped shapes derive
everything, which is why nothing noticed.

Two `chain` occurrences remain in the design template, which the vocabulary lint
does not scan. Round eight changed the one the lint reported and swept nothing,
which is the third round in a row to work a report rather than a class.

The public overview still carries an `AUTO-GENERATED` banner and a generation
date although round seven established it has no template, and three of its
snippets contradict source. The README does not mention this crate at all and
its build section lost a paragraph.
