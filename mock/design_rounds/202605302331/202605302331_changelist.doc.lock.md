## Intent

Reframe the engine DESIGN to describe the FLAT execution plan, the load-bearing slice of the store-backed-plan arc. The nested const-array `ExecutionPlan` measured 11 MB at `DefaultPlanDims` (dominated by the dense `Phases x TrunksPerPhase x ComponentsPerTrunk` fiber nesting, 32768 reserved fiber slots), so `compute_execution_plan::<DefaultPlanDims>` overflows the stack and blocks the topological-dispatch slice (#647). The phase / trunk / fiber tree flattens CSR-style into plan-wide-sized flat pools: the dense nesting collapses to the `D::Fibers` flat pool (64 slots at the default budget), dissolving the overflow.

This is the first of two rounds on the arc. This round ships the flatten (a self-contained, cross-crate-free change that dissolves the overflow and unblocks #647). The store-backing (moving the flat columns onto the scheduler-owned columnar store, with the cross-crate provider wiring and the `Send`/`Sync` lifecycle) is the designed follow-on, recorded in the engine BACKLOG under the unified-storage arc. The de-risk sketch (`mock/research/sketches/202605302345_store-backed-flat-plan`, verdict WORKS) validated both the flatten and the eventual store-backing; the staging is the "ship the load-bearing piece, follow on with the cross-crate layer" decomposition.

The api DESIGN is unchanged (`ColumnStorage` shipped in PR #112; the engine consumes it in the follow-on round, not this one).

## The design

`ExecutionPlan` stays a value type but stops nesting. It holds three flat arrays sized by the plan-wide dimensions: `phases` (`D::Phases`), a flat `trunks` pool (`D::Trunks`), and a flat `fibers` pool (`D::Fibers`). `Phase` reshapes in place: it drops the nested `trunks: Array<Trunk>` field and gains a `(trunk_offset, trunk_count)` range into the flat `trunks` pool (keeping `id`, `strategy`, `config`). `Trunk` reshapes in place: it drops `components: Array<TrunkComponent>` and gains a `(fiber_offset, fiber_count)` range into the flat `fibers` pool (keeping `id`). `Fiber` is reused unchanged (its units stay inline). The fiber-formation step (`project_fiber_components`) writes the flat `trunks` and `fibers` pools with running plan-wide ids; the runner assigns the per-phase CSR ranges; `fiber_grouping_from_trunks` reads the flat `fibers` pool. The step functions keep their analysis logic; only the assembly target changes, from nested arrays to flat pools. `TrunkComponent`, `Branch`, and `Bridge` stay defined as forward fan-out / fan-in vocabulary (not wired into the flat representation yet).
