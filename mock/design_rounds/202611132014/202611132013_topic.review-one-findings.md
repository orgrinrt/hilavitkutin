## Finding 1: the reach boundary on the per-core record-slice path

The reviewer's words: "the reach phase cut is ignored on the unit-outer parallel path. Once a carrier holds any accumulator, every worker runs the whole carrier over its own record slice with no barrier between units, so a `Reach<Column<T>, N>` reader reads a neighbour record another core's writer is still writing: a data race". Reproduced three times out of three with 20000 records on four workers, and the control without the accumulator passes.

The design already says what has to hold. In the engine's design, under the value function and the close: "a read-after-write edge into a read with a reach is a phase boundary: the writer's whole range lands, on every core, before the reader's phase starts." The const grouping opens the phase (`compute_phases_waist`), and the phase loop of `run_parallel` crosses a waist barrier between phases, so that path keeps it. A carrier with an accumulator does not take the phase loop. `worker_accum_unit_outer` hands each core a slice of the record range and walks the whole carrier over it once, and nothing stands between a writer on one core and the reach reader on the next. So the code is short of the design, and the design is not wrong.

Two repairs were named by the reviewer: refuse a plain-column reach in such a carrier at `build`, or a barrier across the cores ahead of the reader on that path. The design asks for the second. Refusing would narrow what a consumer may register beyond what the design says, and the barrier is the thing the design sentence describes.

What the barrier costs is what it has to be measured against, and only a carrier that has both an accumulator and a reach read behind a writer pays it. The cut positions are a property of the registered units, so they fold at compile time as the phases do, and a carrier with no such reader keeps the walk it has today.

One neighbouring case the design does not name: a unit that writes a plain column listed after a unit that reads that column with a reach. On the per-core path the reader of one core can still be reading across the slice edge while the next core has gone on to the later writer. The design sentence speaks only of the writer ahead of the reader. This round probes that case as a test and does not decide it: if it is red, it is a ticket and the test is committed marked with it.

## Finding 3: a buffered write that only lands where the value differs survives

The mutant: store on the live side only where the new value differs from the frozen one. `buffered_column`, `maintenance_units` and `unit_bodies` stay green under it, since every test writes a value that differs at every record. The design is explicit that the write lands "whether the two differ or not, since the live side does not hold the frozen value to begin with". The reviewer's probe writes a value that differs at even records and equals the frozen side at odd ones, in both modes, and checks the frozen side after one pass. It is green on the code as it stands and has to be shown red under the mutant.

## Finding 4: the reach rule folds at the default dimensions

`ReachRule::HOLDS` names `DefaultPlanDims` itself. `build` plans at `DefaultPlanDims` too, in `Buildable`, so the two agree today and nothing slips past. They agree by both spelling the same name, and the reviewer's point stands as drift: a build at other dimensions would keep checking the rule at the default ones. The rule takes the dimensions as a parameter and `build` hands it the ones it plans at.

## Finding 5: `Flip::turned` has no safety comment at the type

`Flip::turned` is a `Cell` the workers read and the main thread writes at the boundary. It is sound by the sequence of `frame_publish` and `frame_await_done` and by the per-core copies rebuilt each frame. The type says none of that. It gets the comment.
