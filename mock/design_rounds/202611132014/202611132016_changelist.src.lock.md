## CHANGE: the unit-outer path is cut and crosses a barrier ahead of a reach read
Files: `mock/crates/hilavitkutin/src/plan/reach_cut.rs`, `mock/crates/hilavitkutin/src/plan/unit_masks.rs`, `mock/crates/hilavitkutin/src/plan/mod.rs`, `mock/crates/hilavitkutin/src/plan/grouping.rs`, `mock/crates/hilavitkutin/src/scheduler/worker.rs`, `mock/crates/hilavitkutin/tests/reach_across_cores.rs`.
Finding 1 of the review. `plan::reach_cut` is new. `reach_cuts` folds, per carrier position, whether a consumer unit reads a plain column with a reach behind a writer of it, from the same masks and the same `reach_behind_a_writer` the phases are cut by, and says beside the mask whether any bit is set. `cut_segments` walks the consumer units in segments cut ahead of each such position and hands each segment out with whether it is the last. The two folds it starts from, `masks_of` and `reaches_of`, move unchanged out of `plan/grouping.rs` into the private `plan/unit_masks.rs`, where both the grouping and the cuts reach them. `plan/grouping.rs` was over the file size line and is the shorter for it.

`worker_accum_unit_outer` asks for the cuts. With none it walks as it did. With any, each core runs one segment through `run_gated` over its slice and crosses `waist_barrier` after every segment but the last. A core with no slice, and every core but the first in a pass with no records, runs nothing and still crosses each barrier, since the barrier counts every core. The publish of the per-core accumulator counts moves to `publish_accum_counts`, which both walks call.

The tests are in `tests/reach_across_cores.rs`, a file of their own: the probe is about a plain column, and `tests/buffered_column.rs` is over six hundred lines already. `a_reach_read_waits_for_the_writer_ahead_of_it_on_every_core` was committed ahead of the fix and was red there, with the reader of the second slice reading record 4999 before its writer landed it. It is green with the fix, and `the_phase_loop_keeps_the_boundary_ahead_of_a_reach_read` is its control. `the_boundary_holds_pass_after_pass` runs three passes over the same barrier. `a_core_with_no_slice_still_meets_the_others_at_the_cut` and `a_pass_with_no_records_ends_on_the_cut_walk` were shown failing with the barrier crossed only by the cores that run: the pass never ended and the run was stopped by a timeout. `the_walk_is_cut_ahead_of_a_reach_read_behind_a_writer_and_nowhere_else` was shown failing with the fold cutting at every consumer unit, and `the_segments_fall_between_the_cuts` with a cut closing a segment that holds nothing.

The doc changelist left open whether a writer listed behind a reach reader of its column needs a boundary of its own. It does not come up: `build` refuses such a registration as `NonTopologicalRegistration`, with or without an accumulator in the carrier, and `a_writer_listed_behind_a_reach_read_of_its_column_is_refused` holds that. So the boundary ahead of the reader is the only one, and no ticket was needed.

## CHANGE: the buffered write is held where the value equals the frozen one
Files: `mock/crates/hilavitkutin/tests/buffered_column.rs`.
Finding 3 of the review. No source changes: `land_compared` already stores the value on the live side whatever the frozen side holds. `a_value_equal_to_the_frozen_one_still_lands_on_the_live_side` is new and runs under both buffer modes. It was shown failing in both with the store made only where the value differs from the frozen one, at record 1.

## CHANGE: the reach rule folds at the dimensions it is handed
Files: `mock/crates/hilavitkutin/src/scheduler/reach_rule.rs`, `mock/crates/hilavitkutin/src/scheduler/buildable.rs`, `mock/crates/hilavitkutin/src/scheduler/mod.rs`.
Finding 4 of the review. `ReachRule` takes the plan dimensions as a fourth parameter and folds at their unit and store capacities. `Buildable::RULES` hands it `DefaultPlanDims`, the dimensions its `plan` computes the plan at, so the two are named in one place.

What the finding feared does not happen with the fold as it is: a bundle with more units than the capacity it is folded at indexes past the mask arrays and fails the build, so no schedule got past the rule by being wide. The two could still drift apart once `build` plans at other dimensions, which is what the parameter closes.

A departure: `ReachRule` and `HOLDS` become public, and `scheduler` exports the type. The design names the assertion and where it folds, and does not say the type can be named from outside. It is public so that a doctest can force the rule at dimensions other than the default. The new `compile_fail` doctest folds two units at dimensions with room for one, and was shown failing, by compiling, with the rule taking the parameter and still folding at the default. The doctest beside it folds the same two at the default dimensions and builds. The design text is owed in the next doc phase (ticket bdf787ead0b5).
