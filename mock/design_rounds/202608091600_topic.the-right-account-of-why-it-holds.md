# The right account of why it holds, and the sweep round six did not do

**Date:** 2026-08-09
**Phase:** TOPIC
**Scope:** `hilavitkutin-sym`, `hilavitkutin-str`, `docs/`

Round six closed the width class. The sixth review confirms that by construction
and then makes a sharper point: **the recorded reason it holds is wrong**, and
this class has been mis-explained at every one of its relocations. That is the
substance of this round.

Two lesser threads follow it. Round six locked a false measurement, which is the
fourth consecutive round to do so and the first where it was mine. And it fixed
what the fifth review listed without sweeping what the fifth review left.

## One: `FieldWidth` is not what closes it, and the doc says it is

The rustdoc at `shape.rs:115-126` credits `FieldWidth` over `Self::Kind`,
arguing that `get_kind` returns it and its body returns what the bitfield hands
back. **Nothing enforces that body.** An accessor may convert.

A layout declaring `type Kind = Bits<9, Hot>` over a genuine five-bit field,
converting in both directions, satisfies every signature. Under it all six
shape-level width laws pass, `every_shape_declares_the_widths_its_layout_actually_has`
included, because each of them reads the declared width against the declared
type and the two agree.

What catches it is the accessor exercise round six added: the round-trip law
fails when a nine-bit maximum is written and a five-bit value reads back, and the
whole-handle law fails because four bits belong to no field.

So the class is closed, by the new suite rather than by the trait. The doc must
say which, because **a wrong account of what guarantees what is exactly what
moved this defect four times.** Each round named a mechanism, believed the
naming, and stopped looking.

### And the same defect is in the suite that caught it

`tests/layout.rs` instantiates its macro with literal widths, `Bits<5, Hot>` and
`Bits<26, Hot>`, which restate what the layout already says. They are constrained
rather than free, since a mismatch is a type error against the real accessors, so
this is the weak form rather than the silent one. It is still a hand-written
number saying something another artifact says.

Deriving them from `<L as SymLayoutOps>::Kind` removes the restatement, and the
catch survives: with the widths derived, the converting layout above fails the
round-trip law at runtime instead of failing to compile. Later rather than
earlier, and by a law that constrains rather than a number that agrees.

## Two: a false measurement, locked, for the fourth round running

The locked src changelist claims `cargo fmt --check` reports zero diffs. It
reports **five**, all import ordering in `hilavitkutin-str`, all pre-existing,
all of which that changelist claims were normalised.

The cause is worth recording because it is a trap rather than carelessness. The
check was run as `cargo fmt -p A -p B -- --check`, which reported nothing, while
`cargo fmt --check -p A -p B` reports five. The verification command and the
gate's command were not the same command, and the one that was run was the one
that agreed.

No `lint:allow` line moves under any of the five, so the risk here is to the
record rather than to the code. That does not make it smaller: four consecutive
rounds have now locked a claim that measurement contradicts, and the whole point
of a locked changelist is that a later reader can trust it without re-measuring.

## Three: what round six did not sweep

The fifth review left three items round six did not name at all.

`DESIGN.md.tmpl:175` shows `SymKind` deriving `Hash`; `kind.rs:24` does not.
`kind.rs:28` documents the generic `SymKind::new` as building "a tag from its
3-bit value", which is the default shape's width standing in for every shape's.

And `docs/HILAVITKUTIN_SYM_OVERVIEW.md` states the three-bit tag as definitive
and knows nothing of `SymShape` or `Generator::at`. Round six left it, on the
reasoning that it is a rendered artifact of an owed migration. **That reasoning
was wrong: no `.tmpl` source for it exists**, so it is hand-written, and it is a
public document describing the exact behaviour this branch changes.

The pattern under all three is one thing: the round worked the list of what it
was fixing rather than the list of what was outstanding. A review's findings are
instances; the outstanding set is the thing to clear.
