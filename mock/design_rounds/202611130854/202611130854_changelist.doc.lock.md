## The kinds, and why these three

The registry has three namespaces: `subject`, `tenet` and `question`.

kaski keeps its canon as `tenet` rows under `subject` rows, and the shared workflow tools (`canon`, `quote-check`, and the lints that hold tenet and subject rows) read exactly those two namespaces by name, with `standing` as a closed set of `provisional`, `ratified` and `demoted`. Declaring the two as the tools read them means `cargo mock canon find`, `subject`, `related` and `check` work on these rows the moment the pack is declared here, with no second schema to migrate.

arvo splits what kaski keeps in one kind: a `ruling` with a rung, a `proposal`, an `obligation` and a `question`. Three of the four carry something this record needs, and each fits without a kind of its own.

The ruling and proposal split is the difference between op's own text and an agent-written proposal. Here it is a field, `origin`, with `stated` and `proposed`, and `written_by` beside it for a proposal. A kind per origin would put one intent in two places when a later roadmap restates the founding spec, and the tools read one kind.

The obligation is a demand some design must meet, tracked for coverage. Nothing in this round tracks coverage of designs against rows, so the kind is not declared. It can be added when a design round first declares the rows it descends from.

The question is kept as a kind. The record states some things two ways and leaves others open, and op answered several questions on 13 November among options that would otherwise be lost. A question binds nothing, so it is not a tenet with a different standing, and its rows sit under `mock/registry/question/`, outside `canon_paths`.

Two things differ from the brief's wording, both because of the closed set the tools read. The brief asks for `standing` to be `stated` or `proposed`. `standing` here keeps the tools' three words and every row of this round is `provisional`, since nothing is ratified by a transcription; `stated` and `proposed` are the values of `origin`. And the passage a row rests on is two fields in the shape `quote-check` reads, a locator in `source` and the lines in `quote`, so the tool can check every row against the file.

## Changes

### CHANGE 1: `mockspace.toml`, the canon declaration

`canon_paths = ["mock/registry/*.toml", "mock/registry/tenet/*.toml"]`. The three namespaces are declared with their fields. `subject` and `tenet` carry every field the pack's declaration has. `tenet` adds `mechanism`, `source`, `quote`, `rev`, `origin` and `written_by`. `question` has `asks`, `options`, `state`, `answer`, `answered`, `source`, `quote`, `rev`, `subject`, `keywords` and `related`. `[lints.no-superseded-citation]` gains `field = "superseded_by"`, which the lint asks for once the registry has rows; no severity is set or changed.

### CHANGE 2: `mock/registry/`, the rows

`subject.toml` holds the subjects. `tenet/<subject>.toml` holds the tenets by subject, continued in `<subject>-2.toml` and on where a file would pass the size limit. `question/<subject>.toml` holds the questions. Every tenet cites its passage by path and line and quotes it exactly; its `what` says no more than the passage does; a technique or concrete shape the passage names is in `mechanism`. Rows from the founding consolidation spec, the principles and rulings the record quotes are `origin = "stated"`; rows from later agent-written rounds, roadmaps and memos are `origin = "proposed"` and name their writer. Where two passages disagree both rows exist and a `question` row names the disagreement and links them.

### CHANGE 3: `.gitattributes`, the registry merge driver

`cargo mock` writes the line that merges registry files by namespace and id once the registry exists.

### CHANGE 4: `mock/research/202611130850_the-record-read-whole/`

`LEDGER.md` is the read: one line per file of the record, with the files still unread listed as such. `OP-ANSWERS.md` holds the questions put to op on 13 November 2026 with their options and his answers, word for word. `ANSWERS.md` answers eight questions from the record with rows cited and passages quoted. Research files need no round; they are listed for the same reason as above.
