## CHANGE: `tests/gate2_accumulator.rs` GAINS a second trunk in the accumulator's phase

File: `mock/crates/hilavitkutin/tests/gate2_accumulator.rs`

A column-disjoint producer, `SideWu`, registered alongside `KeepWu` so the phase carries two trunks
instead of one. `KeepWu` still appends at most once per record; `SideWu` only writes its own column,
so it neither reads nor writes the accumulator.

Two trunks takes the phase off the `tphase == 1` branch and onto the trunk-rank walk, where the core
owning `KeepWu`'s trunk walks every record rather than a slice, while its accumulator region is
still sized from the slice.

The test asserts the parallel result matches the single-core reference, the same contract the
existing accumulator test uses. What it is really probing is whether the configuration can be built
and run at all.

Three outcomes, all informative, and the changelist commits to none of them:

- **Builds and matches.** The region arithmetic is safe under trunk-rank ownership, either because
  something bounds the appends or because the region is larger than this fixture needs. R1a stays
  small. The test ships green as a regression.
- **Builds and corrupts, or trips a bounds assert.** The region is undersized under trunk-rank
  ownership. R1a carries a memory-model change, and this test is the case that proves it, shipping
  catalogued-red against the fix.
- **Does not build.** The plan or the type system refuses an accumulator in a multi-trunk phase,
  which answers the question outright and is the best outcome: the constraint is structural rather
  than a bound anyone has to maintain. The test is then rewritten as a compile-fail fixture or
  dropped in favour of documenting the constraint.

**Outcome: the first one. It builds and matches**, and tracing why answered a larger question than
the round asked.

`scheduler/mod.rs:873` routes an accumulator-bearing carrier to `worker_accum_unit_outer` and
`continue`s the loop. Such a carrier never reaches `run_core_phase`. And `worker_accum_unit_outer`
begins at `:933`, which places the entire record-slice apparatus, `per` at `:960`, `lo` and `hi`,
`run_this` at `:966`, `rebase_accums` at `:971`, inside it.

So record-slice ownership belongs to the accumulator path alone. The non-accumulator path calls
`run_core_phase` unconditionally for every core on every phase, and states the invariant at `:913`:
"every worker participates in each waist, even one that owned no trunk this phase".

The two ownership models are mutually exclusive paths, not competing gates on one path. They cannot
disagree. That retires the premise behind the R1a/R1b split introduced earlier today, and R1 returns
to what r7 originally said: a deletion that strands nothing. Task #695 is deleted; the sketch and
r7's R1 entry are corrected in the same commit range.

The test ships green as a regression. It found no defect, and it pins a property that had been
asserted from argument three times without evidence.

Verification: `cargo test -p hilavitkutin --test gate2_accumulator` reports two passing cases.

## Not in scope

Any change to region sizing, participation, or the N-way branch. This round observes.
