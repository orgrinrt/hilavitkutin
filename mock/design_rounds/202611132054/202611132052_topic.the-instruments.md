## What exists to build on

The sketch `202611132010_oracle-over-record-op` holds a one-thread runner written against `hilavitkutin-api` alone. It keeps every store in storage the caller lends, gathers a unit's read product at one record, calls `RecordOp::apply` and stores what came back. It never calls `execute`, so it needs no context type. Its five units and its hand model agree over six frames, and with its one mutant switched on the test fails. The sketch is the working model for the oracle here.

The branch at `5603f85` holds the laws as comparisons of word slices with a verdict that names where a law first failed, mutant stores that each law has to fail, a ledger of content hashes with a lint over it, three lints that keep a frozen crate written as a consumer could write it, and a tool that compares digest streams between the host and wasm under node. All of it is written on that branch's own contract crate, which this repository does not take.

The engine has three run entries, `run`, `run_parallel` and `run_fused`, and nothing they are all held to. The record of 13 November has the lead designer's finding on this: "Three run entries compute differently: the parallel path skips the incremental skip, and `run_fused` loses accumulator appends."

## The frame's meaning the oracle takes

The oracle states one meaning and takes it from the designs, not from what the engine does today.

At the start of a frame every virtual is unfired and every accumulator is empty. The units run in the sequence they are listed, each over every record. A unit with the schedule `On<V>` runs only when `V` was fired earlier in the frame. A read of a plain column sees what is in it at that moment, so a unit listed after a writer of the column reads what the writer wrote. A read of a buffered column sees the frozen side whatever was written in the frame. A write to a buffered column lands on the live side. After the last unit each buffered column that was written brings its live side in, by a copy under `Snapshot` and by trading the sides under `Exchange`; one that nobody wrote is left alone.

Every `Always` unit runs every frame. The engine may rest a unit under the incremental skip, and its design says why that is allowed: "a unit whose input cone did not change produces identical output whether it runs or not, so skipping is correctness-preserving." A runner that rests nothing is therefore what the skip has to equal, and where the engine rests a unit and the stores come out different, the skip is what is wrong.

An accumulator's appends come out in the sequence of the records. The engine's design says the same of its own merge: the merged prefix is "byte-identical to the single-core unit-outer `run()` append when nothing was refused", each core walking an ascending slice.

The oracle answers only for unit lists in which every unit that fires `V` stands ahead of every `On<V>` unit. The engine's design gives a virtual fired behind its consumer a meaning across passes (the consumer runs on the next pass), and the oracle does not restate that: it refuses such a frame with a value that says so. It also refuses an accumulator append past the lent storage, and a world one of whose columns holds fewer records than it was told.

## Laws and mutants

Three laws come over from the branch, as functions over recorded words that return a verdict. The first says a run equals its model, word for word, frame after frame. The second says runs that differ only in how the records were split and visited are equal. The third says a frame leaves the frozen sides as it found them until the boundary, and that what it commits does not depend on what the live sides held before it.

The branch's mutants were stores. The oracle here owns its stores, so the mutants become ways the oracle itself keeps a frame wrongly: reading the live side, writing the frozen side, bringing in a side nobody wrote, leaving out the last record, keeping an accumulator's appends or a virtual's flag across the boundary, running a triggered unit that was not triggered. Each is a named type the oracle is generic over, shipped beside the faithful one, so that anything held to the laws can be shown failing them.

The second law needs two faithful runs that differ in the visiting, so the oracle takes the visiting as a parameter too: ascending, which is the reference, and descending.

## The authors

The author units are a consumer's crate: value-form units over their own column types, naming the engine's context type as every unit does today (ticket 3df7466ff25a is with the coordinator on that line), beside a hand model of the frame in plain values. The brief asks for one neighbour reader on two axes and one emitter of each trigger kind. I read "trigger kind" as the three ways a unit's output makes something else happen: an append to an accumulator, a fired virtual with an `On<V>` unit behind it, and the changed mark, under both `Written` and `Differs`.

The compile-fail twin "a unit naming an engine type" does not exist while a unit has to name `CtxFor`; the report says so and names the ticket.

## Where the scheduler is expected to disagree

Three places are known before the test is written. The incremental skip can rest a unit that writes a plain column in place (the marker at ticket 8e2e2326d588). `run_parallel` dispatches every unit where `run` rests some (ticket 8d8ccf1d9da2). `run_fused` and appends were the subject of the compile-fail pair `appending_unit_fused` in round one. Whatever the test finds is committed as it stands, ignored with its ticket where it fails, and the law is not changed to fit.
